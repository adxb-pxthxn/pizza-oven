#### 1.3 Hearth Physics, Diffusivity & Differential Expansion
*   **Why stone thickness governs recovery time (`[Modelled]`)**:
    *   From the physics constants, cordierite has thermal conductivity $k = 2.2\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, density $\rho = 2100\text{ kg/m}^3$, specific heat $c_p = 950\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, and thermal effusivity $e_s = \sqrt{k \rho c_p} = 2095\text{ J/(m}^2\cdot\text{K}\cdot\text{s}^{1/2})$.
    *   Thermal diffusivity of cordierite is:
        $$\alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c_p} = \frac{2.2}{2100 \times 950} = 1.103 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
    *   When $25^\circ\text{C}$ dough ($e_d = 1259$) lands on a $480^\circ\text{C}$ cordierite stone, the instantaneous interface temperature is:
        $$T_{\text{interface}} = \frac{e_s T_s + e_d T_d}{e_s + e_d} = \frac{2095(480) + 1259(25)}{2095 + 1259} = 309.2^\circ\text{C}$$
    *   Over a $90\text{ s}$ bake, the cooling wave penetrates a characteristic depth into the stone of:
        $$\delta \approx 2\sqrt{\alpha t} = 2\sqrt{1.103 \times 10^{-6} \times 90} = 0.0199\text{ m} \approx 20\text{ mm}$$
    *   `[Modelled]` Conclusion: In the Arc Lite and Tread ($12\text{ mm}$ stone), the cooling wave hits the bottom of the stone before a single $90\text{ s}$ pizza finishes baking, requiring a recovery pause between pizzas. A $20\text{ mm}$ stone matches the $90\text{ s}$ penetration depth. The Dome XL's $30\text{ mm}$ stone acts as a semi-infinite reservoir during a bake, but its characteristic through-thickness diffusion time is $\tau \approx L^2 / \alpha = (0.030)^2 / (1.103 \times 10^{-6}) = 816\text{ s}$ ($13.6\text{ min}$), requiring $\sim 3\tau \approx 41\text{ min}$ for top-to-bottom thermal equilibration.
    *   `[Published]` vs `[Modelled]` Dome XL heat-up reconciliation: The US comparison tool claims $40\text{–}45\text{ min}$, while the EU spec sheet lists $15\text{ min}$ to $300^\circ\text{C}$, $30\text{ min}$ to $400^\circ\text{C}$, and $60\text{ min}$ to $500^\circ\text{C}$. **Builder rule:** Use **$60\text{ min}$** for a fully soaked $30\text{ mm}$ floor; $40\text{–}45\text{ min}$ only brings the top skin of the stone to $500^\circ\text{C}$.

*   **Differential Thermal Expansion (`[Modelled]`)**:
    *   Heating the 304 stainless steel inner dome ($\alpha = 18 \times 10^{-6}\text{ K}^{-1}$) from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $550^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = 530\text{ K}$) vs. the cordierite stone ($\alpha = 2.8 \times 10^{-6}\text{ K}^{-1}$) from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $500^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = 480\text{ K}$):
        *   **Arc Lite / Tread ($380\text{ mm}$ span)**: Inner 304 grows by $18 \times 10^{-6} \times 380 \times 530 = 3.63\text{ mm}$. Cordierite grows by $2.8 \times 10^{-6} \times 380 \times 480 = 0.51\text{ mm}$.
        *   **Dome XL ($724\text{ mm}$ span)**: Inner 304 grows by $18 \times 10^{-6} \times 724 \times 530 = 6.91\text{ mm}$. Cordierite grows by $2.8 \times 10^{-6} \times 724 \times 480 = 0.97\text{ mm}$.
    *   **Builder rule:** Never weld the inner 304 core rigidly to the cold outer shell—use slotted tabs or slip joints at the mouth arch. Leave a minimum $4\text{ mm}$ perimeter expansion gap around the cordierite stone so the steel tray does not crush the stone as the steel cools faster than the refractory.

### 3. Australian Gas Regulations: AS/NZS 5601 vs. Appliance Certification

Building in Adelaide requires separating two distinct regulatory domains:

1.  **AS/NZS 5601.1 (Gas Installations)**:
    *   `[Inferred]` from Australian regulatory practice: **AS/NZS 5601.1** governs fixed gas installations, pipe sizing, cylinder storage, ventilation, and appliance clearances from combustible structures (note the `[Published]` Gozney clearances: $0.3\text{ m}$ back and sides, $1.0\text{ m}$ front, never under or on a combustible structure, outdoor use only).
    *   You must **never** connect an uncertified DIY oven to a home's fixed natural-gas or plumbed LPG reticulation line. In South Australia (regulated by the Office of the Technical Regulator, OTR), connecting an uncertified appliance to fixed plumbing is illegal and voids building insurance.

2.  **Appliance Certification (AGA / SANZ — AS/NZS 5263.1.7)**:
    *   `[Published]` The Dome XL Gen 2 EU spec sheet explicitly lists **AGA** and **SANZ** alongside CE and ETL.
    *   `[Inferred]` In Australia, commercial sale or distribution of any gas appliance requires Type Testing and certification to **AS/NZS 5263.1.7** (Domestic outdoor gas cooking appliances) by a JAS-ANZ accredited body (such as the Australian Gas Association, AGA, or Global-Mark—note **GM** on the Tread spec sheet), costing tens of thousands of dollars in lab emissions (CO/$\text{CO}_2$ ratio), wind-extinction, surface-temperature, and rain testing.
    *   **What this means for Adib:** You do **not** need appliance certification to build a single, non-commercial, portable LPG prototype connected via a flexible hose to a portable patio gas bottle (minimum $5\text{ kg}$ bottle `[Published]` recommendation; standard Australian $4\text{ kg}$ or $8.5\text{ kg}$ LCC27 swap bottle in practice) for your own backyard use. You **do** need full AGA/JAS-ANZ certification if you ever sell, rent, gift, or commercially operate the oven.
    *   Even on a personal prototype, buy a **certified burner + FFD valve train**, a **certified Australian $2.75\text{ kPa}$ ($11\text{ in WC}$) regulator**, and an **AS/NZS 1869 Class A/C compliant hose** with an **LCC27** connection. Never drill your own brass jets, never bypass the Flame Failure Device thermocouple, and never enclose the LPG bottle inside an unventilated base cabinet.

### 4. Recommended First Oven for Adib: "The Adelaide Folded-Vault 14"

Do not waste time trying to hydroform or deep-draw compound-curved shells like the Tread or Arc Lite, and do not start with a $73\text{ kg}$ multi-fuel monster like the Dome XL. Build a **press-brake faceted 14-inch portable tabletop LPG oven** that takes the best functional lessons from all three ovens while fixing the thin-stone limitation of the Arc Lite and Tread.

#### 4.1 Functional & Envelope Specification (`[Modelled]` Design)
*   **Target Pizza & Floor Temp**: $12\text{ to }14\text{ in}$ ($300\text{–}355\text{ mm}$) Neapolitan pizza at a $450\text{–}500^\circ\text{C}$ floor temperature with zero recovery lag between pizzas.
*   **Hearth Stone**: **$380 \times 380 \times 20\text{ mm}$ cordierite kiln shelf** (wet-cut from a standard $400 \times 400 \times 20\text{ mm}$ pottery shelf).
    *   *Why $20\text{ mm}$ instead of $12\text{ mm}$ or $30\text{ mm}$?* From Section 1.3, $20\text{ mm}$ equals the exact $90\text{ s}$ thermal diffusion depth ($\delta = 19.9\text{ mm}$) in cordierite. It prevents the mid-bake temperature crash of the $12\text{ mm}$ Arc Lite/Tread stones while heating up in $\sim 25\text{ min}$ instead of the $60\text{ min}$ required by the Dome XL's $30\text{ mm}$ slab. *   **Internal Chamber ($W \times D \times H$)**: **$435 \times 410 \times 155\text{ mm}$** ($380\text{ mm}$ stone + $50\text{ mm}$ side slot on the left for the lateral burner + $5\text{ mm}$ thermal expansion clearance).
*   **Mouth Arch ($W \times H$)**: **$370 \times 90\text{ mm}$**.
    *   Mouth-to-apex ratio: $90 / 155 = 0.581$ (sits right between the $0.567$ of the Arc Lite/Tread and the $0.588$ of the Dome XL, giving easy peel access for a $14\text{ in}$ pie while trapping the rolling roof flame).
*   **Insulation Stack**:
    *   **Over the inner vault**: $50\text{ mm}$ of $128\text{ kg/m}^3$ AES bio-soluble blanket (Superwool).
    *   **Under the stone**: $25\text{ mm}$ rigid calcium silicate board ($k \approx 0.065\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) + $12\text{ mm}$ AES wool pad beneath it.
    *   *Steady-state heat-loss check (`[Modelled]`)*: With an internal surface area of $\sim 0.52\text{ m}^2$ at $500^\circ\text{C}$, outer skin at $55^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = 445\text{ K}$), and $50\text{ mm}$ insulation ($k \approx 0.08\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$), conductive shell loss is $Q_{\text{cond}} \approx k A \Delta T / L = 0.08 \times 0.52 \times 445 / 0.050 = 370\text{ W}$. #### 4.4 When Should You Spend the Extra Money to Scale to a 16-Inch Floor?
Going from a $14\text{ in}$ ($380\text{ mm}$) floor to a $16\text{ in}$ ($450\text{ mm}$) floor is not a linear $18\%$ scale-up; it triggers four step-changes in cost and physics (`[Modelled]`):
1.  **One-piece kiln shelves top out or get heavy**: A $450 \times 450 \times 25\text{ mm}$ cordierite stone weighs $10.6\text{ kg}$ alone ($40\%$ more area plus extra thickness to prevent sagging/cracking). Total oven weight jumps from $\sim 18.5\text{ kg}$ to $> 32\text{ kg}$, crossing the threshold from a one-person portable oven to a two-person semi-permanent cart oven.
2.  **Burner power must jump from $\sim 4.2\text{ kW}$ to $\sim 6.5\text{–}7.5\text{ kW}$**: A $16\text{ in}$ pizza requires at least a $430 \times 105\text{ mm}$ mouth opening. Radiative and convective heat loss out of that wider mouth scales with area, meaning a single $3.7\text{–}4.5\text{ kW}$ lateral burner will leave the far side of a $450\text{ mm}$ stone cold.
3.  **Sheet-metal stock sizes**: A $14\text{ in}$ vault unfolds inside a standard $600\text{ mm}$ offcut width; a $16\text{ in}$ vault plus $75\text{ mm}$ insulation walls forces you into full $1200 \times 2400\text{ mm}$ sheets and heavier $2.0\text{ mm}$ 304 inner gauge to prevent roof sag.
4.  **Verdict**: Only spend the extra $\text{AUD } 200\text{–}350$ to build a $16\text{ in}$ floor if you specifically bake **New York-style $16\text{ in}$ slicing pies**, want to roast **two cast-iron skillets side-by-side**, or plan to leave the oven permanently on a wheeled patio cart. For Neapolitan pizza, a $14\text{ in}$ floor bakes faster, uses half the gas, and fits in the boot of a car.

# Terrax — build at Makerspace Adelaide

Four modules: pit, hearth, brow, tunnel. Ready 34.1 kg. The deck is 4 mm mild, 440 × 516, and it is not bolted. Mouth 424 × 185. Hood board is 22 mm. Roof over the pizza is 150 mm.

Machines you may name: Thunder Nova35 and Trotec Q500 for card and MDF templates only. WorkBee for board, MDF jigs, and the jarrah panels. Wood shop: table saw, drop saw, thicknesser. Metal shop: cold cut saw, the welder (process unnamed), the lathe for pins, ferrules, grips, and feet, the mill, hand tools. No brake and no fibre laser at the makerspace. University workshop: TIG, and the brake for the 0.6 mm cowl. Do not weld mild to aluminium. Do not put zinc, galvanised sheet, or zinc-plated screws in the hot box.

Undimensioned sheet is ISO 2768-m. A shared face in the model is a joint, not a press. The only presses are the ferrules in the grips, 0.15 mm radial.

## 1. Templates and jigs

1. Trotec, card: one template per flat blank. Pierce on the waste. Label the module on the template.
2. WorkBee, MDF: a mouth buck, 424 × 185, and a door buck, 421 × 182. These are the fit gauges.
3. WorkBee, MDF: a pit corner square, inside 464 × 660, to hold the walls while they are tacked.

## 2. Pit

Stock: 1.0 mm mild. Floor 464 × 660. Walls 170 mm high. Front wall is the hinge land. Flange strips are 16 mm.

1. Cold cut saw or snips. Deburr. The 1 mm wall is easy to blow through.
2. Tack the four walls to the floor on the MDF square. University workshop, TIG, stitch the corners. Do not run a continuous bead on 1 mm.
3. Tack the 16 mm flange, then stitch it. It is the landing for the hood skirt, with 2 mm clearance later.
4. Hinge arm: 3 mm mild, 16.5 × 115. It butts the front wall and stops 35 mm under the door. University TIG, fillet on the arm side, short stitches. The pin is turned later and stands on this arm.
5. Belly: 12 mm microporous on the WorkBee, then 0.8 mm aluminium tray, 498 × 694. Rivet or screw the tray to the flange in the cool zone only. A light scribe may go on this tray. Nothing is scribed on the cooking plate.
6. Feet: jarrah, turned on the metal lathe, stood on the aluminium belly, not on the steel wall.

Safety: P2 mask for the board. No painted or zinc-coated stock in this box.

## 3. Hearth

1. Floor board, 12 mm, WorkBee. Three microporous pads, 25 × 25 × 10, in a triangle.
2. Deck: 4 mm mild, 440 × 516. Shear or cold saw, not a bend. Leave it floating. Side gap is 12 mm. Do not bolt it, do not weld it, do not scribe it. Season the top face. Food contact is bare seasoned steel.
3. Sill: 4 mm mild, 424 × 80, in front of the flange. The peel lands here.
4. Peel rest: gidgee, 180 × 20 × 16, table saw and thicknesser, in front of the sill and clear of the steel.
5. Bail: jarrah grip, lathe. Stainless posts and a PTFE washer stand it off the sill. Ferrule is the press, 0.15 mm radial. Bore the grip, then turn the ferrule to suit.

## 4. Brow, door, and pintle

The brow is the tall front. Liner is 0.6 mm mild. Skin is 0.6 mm aluminium. Board is 22 mm, then 16 mm of air.

1. Cut the liner flats from the card templates. The cowl is one 45° bend. University workshop, brake, think twice. K-factor about 0.42 for the mild. Corner relief before the bend.
2. University TIG the liner corners. The mouth stays 424 × 185. Check it on the MDF buck before the skin goes on.
3. Door inner: 1.2 mm mild, 421 × 182. Board: 16 mm, inset 8 mm on the edges, WorkBee. Skin: 0.8 mm aluminium. Four stainless spacers, Ø5 × 40, sit in that 8 mm border and butt the skin and the inner plate. They are not a press and they do not pierce the plate.
4. Pintle. Lathe: pin Ø7.2. Mill or lathe: barrel bore Ø8.4, so the radial gap is 0.6 mm. Collar Ø16 sits 0.4 mm under the lower barrel. The upper barrel is on the brow, 1 mm above the door, and the pin ends inside it. The brow lifts off. The leaf is 3 mm mild and bolts to the aluminium. It is not a weld. Use M5, coarse clearance in the aluminium, anti-seize on the stainless threads.
5. Grip pads: 1.5 mm mild, 100 × 20, one each side of the brow skin, the tunnel skin, and the door skin. Clamp pad, skin, pad, and drill through as one stack. Rivet the stack. Do not weld the mild to the aluminium. Posts are Ø5 stainless and stand on the outer pad, 1.5 mm clear of the skin, 7.5 mm inside the pad edge. Weld the posts to the pad, not to the skin. On the door the inner pad sits in the 24 mm air gap and stops 22 mm short of the board.
6. Jarrah panels: 6 mm, 110 × 130, table saw. Stand them 8 mm off the skin on stainless posts and PTFE washers. Do not glue wood to metal.
7. Rope: four strips in the door gap, clear of the skin. Damper: a plate across the flue with a tang out the top.

## 5. Tunnel

1. Same liner, board, air, and aluminium as the brow, on the low body. Soffit is 1.2 mm mild at 150 mm above the deck.
2. Broiler plate on 8 mm stainless rods, over Y 180 to 350, at Z 100. Park it aft for the pizza. The rack hangs 50 mm under the plate.
3. Hood grip: the same pad stack as the brow, on the low roof. This is the lift for the tunnel.
4. Wind-back: 1.2 mm mild, stored under the rear overhang. It stands on the rim when the hood is off.
5. Skirt: 1.2 mm aluminium, 2 mm clear of the pit flange, so the hood drops on and lifts off.

## 6. Close the fits

1. Wood to hot steel stays at least 2 mm. The measured closest pair is the port grip to its post, 3.4 mm. Do not close that gap to make the handle stiffer.
2. Ferrules: 0.15 mm radial press. Everything else that touches is a butt, a bolt, or a running fit.
3. Put the door on the pintle last. Confirm it swings past the arms. The hearth bail and the port panel still share the door's height, so do not force a full turn.
4. The door grip stands on its pad. It pulls the door. Do not hang the tunnel or the brow from it.

## 7. Safety

Hot work stays in the metal shop or the university booth. Deburr every 0.6 mm edge before it is skinned. Microporous and jarrah both need extraction. The burner, valve, regulator, and hose are bought parts. Do not fabricate them.

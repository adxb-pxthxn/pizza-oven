## Thermal method

500°C gas, 20°C air, h_in = 25 W/m²K, h_ext = 10 W/m²K.
R = 1/h_in + sum(thickness_m / k) + 1/h_ext
flux = (500 − 20) / R
T_outer = 20 + flux / h_ext
The temperature at an interface is 20 + flux × (sum of resistances from that interface out to ambient).

k: 304 stainless 16; AES blanket 0.08 at density 128 kg/m³; calcium silicate board 0.065 at 250 kg/m³; hardwood 0.16.
A microporous board, if you use one, is a real class: k about 0.022 to 0.030 W/mK and density about 200 to 300 kg/m³. State the pair you use and keep it consistent. An aerogel blanket class is k about 0.020 and density about 180 kg/m³, and it is the expensive line. Do not invent a k below 0.018.
Aluminium, if the outer skin is aluminium, density 2700 kg/m³, and it is not the food-side liner. The liner that sees the flame stays 304. Outer aluminium is only legitimate where the skin temperature you calculate stays far below the point the alloy softens.

Limits: wood inner face at or under 80°C. A bare metal surface a person might lean on at or under 60°C. Handles a bare hand can close on, cooler than the shell. Compute the grip, do not call a stainless bar on a stainless dome cool because the shell is 55°C.

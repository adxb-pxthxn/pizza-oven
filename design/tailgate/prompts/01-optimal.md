Wood is not a goal. A wood body is allowed only if, after you do the sums, it beats the alternatives on the whole set: 15 inch pizza, circular dome, tailgate and tub of a BYD Shark 6, fast heat-up, certified gas, low cash budget, heirloom finish, one person able to carry it, rain, vibration, and the machines listed in your system instructions. An heirloom can be stainless, spun metal, ceramic, bronze, stone, or timber. Choose the material because it is the right one.

Ignore any urge to meet a wooden aesthetic halfway. A later step will compare your answer with a separate wood-body concept. If you pre-compromise, that comparison is fake.

Return, in this order:

1. The concept in one paragraph. Name it. Say what it is made of and why that won.
2. The two runners-up you discarded, each in a short paragraph with the number that killed it (a mass, a temperature, a diameter, a machine the workshop does not have).
3. Dimensioned description, same items as a fabrication drawing: plan diameter, height, floor, chamber, apex, mouth, mouth/apex, wall stack, legs, handles, stowed envelope, and whether it sits on the lowered tailgate (517 mm deep) or stands on its own legs.
4. Mass table with volumes, densities, and a total. Carry verdict.
5. Heat-up and shell temperature. Show the resistance sum.
6. Build path against the real machine list. Premium details: the three joints or surfaces that make it look expensive, and how they are made with these tools.
7. Certified gas parts, power band, and why that power matches this floor. Wood-fire mode: include it only if it is free; otherwise say no and why.
8. Cost band, AUD, new vs second-hand vs made. Separate the certified gas line.
9. What a careful owner will dislike in year five.
10. A one-screen parts list in millimetres, named solids, ready to model.

One concept only.

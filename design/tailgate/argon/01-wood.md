### 1. Name and Concept

**THE COOPER’S DOME (Project *Cask-430*)**

*Cask-430* is a circular, gas-fired portable Neapolitan pizza oven whose outer body is a 24-stave dry-coopered barrel frustum of 16 mm quarter-sawn Tasmanian Blackwood (*Acacia melanoxylon*), bound by two Belleville-sprung 304 stainless steel tension hoops with machined C36000 brass trunnions, and capped by a satin-brushed 1.2 mm 304 stainless steel dome crown that sheds rain and mouth spill heat over the top of the staves. Inside that timber-and-brass furniture shell sits an industrial furnace core: a TIG-welded 1.5 mm 304 stainless steel inner dome liner, a 430 mm × 20 mm cordierite deck, 75 mm of Morgan Superwool Plus AES blanket, and 50 mm of kerf-slotted calcium silicate board that holds the wood inner face at 69.3°C during a 500°C soak. Too wide at 765 mm to balance on the BYD Shark 6’s 517 mm tailgate, it stands beside the ute on three quick-release bayonet tripod legs that place the hearth at 860 mm off the ground—freeing the lowered tailgate to act as the pizza prep bench—and packs down to a 420 mm stowed height that slides under the Shark 6 tonneau cover with 97 mm to spare.

### 2. Dimensioned Description

#### Plan & Elevation Envelope
*   **Outer body plan diameter:** **765 mm** at the lower belly ($R_{out} = 382.5\text{ mm}$), tapering along a barrel curve to **560 mm** diameter where the wood staves tuck beneath the stainless dome crown drip skirt.
*   **Plan width across side handles:** **855 mm** (two pairs of 45 mm stand-off handles; clears the 1224 mm width between the BYD Shark 6 wheel arches with 369 mm of margin).
*   **Body height (stowed, legs detached):** **420 mm** from the bottom rubber-isolated skid ring to the top of the machined brass crown boss (clears the 460 mm target by 40 mm and the 517 mm Shark 6 tub depth by 97 mm).
*   **Cooking floor:** **430 mm diameter × 20 mm thick** cordierite circular stone. Accommodates a 15-inch (381 mm) pizza with 49 mm of diametral clearance for peel rotation.
*   **Chamber inside diameter:** **480 mm** circular plan ($R_{in} = 240\text{ mm}$). The 430 mm stone is offset 20 mm forward toward the mouth so its front edge meets the stainless landing lip, leaving a 70 mm crescent slot along the rear-left arc for the gas burner port and flame roll baffle.
*   **Chamber apex height above floor:** **165 mm** (55 mm vertical cylindrical wall transitioning into a 110 mm rise oblate dome).
*   **Mouth width and height:** **400 mm wide × 95 mm high** (stadium arch with 65 mm vertical jambs curving into a 95 mm crown, framed by a 145 mm deep TIG-welded 2.0 mm 304 stainless tunnel and upper flame-spill eyebrow).
*   **Mouth-to-apex ratio:** $95\text{ mm} / 165\text{ mm} = \mathbf{0.576}$ (matches the 0.57 ratio of the Gozney Arc Lite to trap radiant dome heat while clearing a 381 mm peel).

#### Wall Stack (Gas Outward at Equator, $R = 240.0\text{ mm}$ to $382.5\text{ mm}$)
1.  **Inner liner:** $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless steel ($R = 240.0 \rightarrow 241.5\text{ mm}$).
2.  **Primary insulation:** $75.0\text{ mm}$ AES refractory blanket ($\rho = 128\text{ kg/m}^3$, $k = 0.08\text{ W/mK}$), installed as two lapped $38\text{ mm}$ layers compressed to $75\text{ mm}$ ($R = 241.5 \rightarrow 316.5\text{ mm}$).
3.  **Secondary rigid insulation:** $50.0\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate insulating board ($\rho = 250\text{ kg/m}^3$, $k = 0.065\text{ W/mK}$), CNC-kerfed polygon staves bonded with refractory mortar and wrapped externally in $0.08\text{ mm}$ aluminium foil vapour barrier tape ($R = 316.5 \rightarrow 366.5\text{ mm}$).
4.  **Outer timber shell:** $16.0\text{ mm}$ quarter-sawn Tasmanian Blackwood coopered staves ($\rho = 640\text{ kg/m}^3$, $k = 0.16\text{ W/mK}$) bound by two $30\text{ mm} \times 2.0\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless tension hoops ($R = 366.5 \rightarrow 382.5\text{ mm}$).

#### Vertical Stack (Ground Upward, Stowed Attitude, $Z = 0$ to $420\text{ mm}$)
1.  **Base skid ring & bayonet sockets:** $15.0\text{ mm}$ ($Z = 0 \rightarrow 15.0\text{ mm}$).
2.  **Base structural pan:** $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless steel with $25\text{ mm}$ upturned chime rim ($Z = 15.0 \rightarrow 16.5\text{ mm}$).
3.  **Under-hearth insulation:** $25.0\text{ mm}$ AES blanket + $50.0\text{ mm}$ load-bearing calcium silicate disc ($Z = 16.5 \rightarrow 91.5\text{ mm}$).
4.  **Cordierite cooking stone:** $20.0\text{ mm}$ ($Z = 91.5 \rightarrow 111.5\text{ mm}$; hearth top at $Z = 111.5\text{ mm}$).
5.  **Internal oven cavity:** $165.0\text{ mm}$ ($Z = 111.5 \rightarrow 276.5\text{ mm}$).
6.  **Inner dome crown liner:** $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless steel ($Z = 276.5 \rightarrow 278.0\text{ mm}$).
7.  **Top dome insulation:** $75.0\text{ mm}$ AES blanket + $50.0\text{ mm}$ segmented calcium silicate cap ($Z = 278.0 \rightarrow 403.0\text{ mm}$).
8.  **Outer dome crown & brass boss:** $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless steel dome cap ($560\text{ mm}$ chord diameter, $115\text{ mm}$ rise from the upper stave chime at $Z = 305\text{ mm}$) plus a $15.8\text{ mm}$ machined brass top trim boss ($Z = 403.0 \rightarrow 420.0\text{ mm}$).

#### Leg, Handle & Tailgate Geometry
*   **Why it does not sit on the tailgate:** The BYD Shark 6 lowered tailgate provides a $517\text{ mm}$ deep horizontal deck. The oven’s $680\text{ mm}$ base pan and $765\text{ mm}$ belly would overhang the tailgate edge by $163\text{–}248\text{ mm}$.
*   **Tripod Stand:** Three removable legs of $32\text{ mm} \times 2.0\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless tube ($780\text{ mm}$ long, splayed outward at $12^\circ$ into a $920\text{ mm}$ ground circle) lock into welded stainless bayonet sockets underneath the base pan with spring-ball hitch pins. Each leg carries a turned Tasmanian Blackwood grip sleeve and an M12 threaded rubber levelling foot.
*   **Operating height & workflow:** On its tripod, the stone floor sits at **860 mm** above ground. The oven stands on the ground $1.2\text{ m}$ behind or beside the open ute; the Shark 6’s $1370\text{ mm} \times 517\text{ mm}$ lowered tailgate becomes a waist-high dough-stretching and topping bench illuminated by the tub light and served by the 230 V tub outlet.
*   **Handles:** Four $4.0\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless brackets bolted to the upper and lower tension hoops at $45^\circ / 135^\circ / 225^\circ / 315^\circ$, carrying two $32\text{ mm}$ diameter $\times 180\text{ mm}$ long turned Tasmanian Blackwood bar handles on the left and right flanks ($45\text{ mm}$ radial stand-off from the staves).
*   **Stowed Envelope in Tub:** **855 mm wide × 785 mm front-to-back (including rear burner guard) × 420 mm tall**. Straps down flat on the rubber tub mat against the front bulkhead using four of the Shark 6’s six tie-down points; the three $780\text{ mm}$ legs stow in a canvas roll alongside one wheel arch.

### 3. Mass Table and Rejected Alternative

#### Rejected Alternative & The Kilograms It Cost
Before arriving at the hybrid 16 mm Tasmanian Blackwood coopered drum + 1.2 mm 304 stainless crown, we evaluated the naive "all-wood dome" version: a full hemisphere-over-cylinder outer shell of **20 mm Jarrah** ($\rho = 840\text{ kg/m}^3$, $A_{net} = 0.964\text{ m}^2$, $V = 19.28\text{ L}$), backed by the original Case C **20 mm dense castable refractory dome** ($\rho = 2200\text{ kg/m}^3$, $A = 0.564\text{ m}^2$, $V = 11.28\text{ L}$) and a **30 mm cordierite floor** ($V = 4.36\text{ L}$).
*   20 mm Full Jarrah Outer Shell: $19.28\text{ L} \times 0.840\text{ kg/L} = \mathbf{16.20\text{ kg}}$ (vs. $6.18\text{ kg}$ Blackwood staves + $2.73\text{ kg}$ 1.2 mm SS crown = $8.91\text{ kg}$ $\rightarrow$ **saves 7.29 kg**, eliminates compound-curved stave waste, and removes flat upper wood grain from rain and rising $180^\circ\text{C}$ mouth spill gas).
*   20 mm Castable Refractory Inner Dome: $11.28\text{ L} \times 2.20\text{ kg/L} = \mathbf{24.82\text{ kg}}$ (replaced by the $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 SS inner dome liner at $3.35\text{ kg}$ $\rightarrow$ **saves 21.47 kg** and cuts preheat time in half while changing wood inner-face temperature by less than $1^\circ\text{C}$).
*   30 mm vs. 20 mm Cordierite Stone: $9.15\text{ kg}$ vs. $6.10\text{ kg}$ $\rightarrow$ **saves 3.05 kg**.
*   **Total rejected mass penalty:** **31.81 kg saved** by rejecting the 20 mm Jarrah full dome, castable liner, and 30 mm stone.

#### One-Person Carry Verdict
**No—this is a two-person lift into and out of the ute tub, and pretending otherwise would injure someone’s back.** Even with the $6.10\text{ kg}$ stone and $3.90\text{ kg}$ leg bundle carried separately in canvas totes, the insulated drum weighs **42.64 kg** and is **765 mm** in diameter. A single strong person can slide it across the Shark 6 tub mat onto the tailgate and pivot it onto its tripod legs in an emergency, but lifting $42.6\text{ kg}$ across a $765\text{ mm}$ span exceeds safe solo ergonomic limits ($\sim 25\text{ kg}$). With two people holding the two $180\text{ mm}$ side bar handles, each person lifts **21.3 kg** with a straight back.

### 4. Heat-Up and Thermal Calculations

#### Burner Specification & Time to 400°C Floor
*   **Burner:** Bought certified commercial twin-tube curved LPG oven burner rated at **6.8 kW** ($24.5\text{ MJ/h}$, $\approx 490\text{ g/h}$ propane at $2.75\text{ kPa}$ via an LCC27 regulator and AS/NZS 1869 Class A hose).
*   **Why 6.8 kW:** Gozney’s Arc Lite uses $3.4\text{ kW}$ for a $0.115\text{ m}^2$ floor; our $430\text{ mm}$ circular floor ($0.145\text{ m}^2$) and $480\text{ mm}$ inner dome have $1.6\times$ the surface area and a $400 \times 95\text{ mm}$ mouth.
*   **Thermal mass to heat during preheat:**
    1.  **Cordierite floor ($6.10\text{ kg}$, $c_p = 1050\text{ J/kgK}$) from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $400^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = 380\text{ K}$):**
        $$Q_{\text{stone}} = 6.10 \times 1050 \times 380 = 2.434\text{ MJ}$$
    2.  **Active 1.5 mm 304 SS inner dome & hearth liner ($4.80\text{ kg}$, $c_p = 500\text{ J/kgK}$) from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $480^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = 460\text{ K}$):**
        $$Q_{\text{liner}} = 4.80 \times 500 \times 460 = 1.104\text{ MJ}$$
    3.  **Inner 25 mm hot-face layer of AES blanket ($1.30\text{ kg}$, $c_p = 1050\text{ J/kgK}$) to mean $\Delta T = 250\text{ K}$:**
        $$Q_{\text{AES}} = 1.30 \times 1050 \times 250 = 0.341\text{ MJ}$$
    *   **Total heat absorbed for a soaked 400°C floor:**
        $$Q_{\text{total}} = 2.434 + 1.104 + 0.341 = \mathbf{3.879\text{ MJ}}$$
*   **Heat-up time:** With an open $400 \times 95\text{ mm}$ mouth, flue spill and radiation losses consume $\sim 68\%$ of gross burner input, leaving a net heat transfer efficiency to the chamber of $\eta \approx 32\%$ ($P_{\text{net}} = 6.8\text{ kW} \times 0.32 = 2.18\text{ kW}$):
    $$t_{\text{open}} = \frac{3,879,000\text{ J}}{2180\text{ W}} = 1779\text{ s} \approx \mathbf{29.7\text{ minutes}}$$
    With the removable half-height stainless preheat baffle hooked across the lower mouth (blocking radiant floor loss while leaving the top $45\text{ mm}$ arch open for secondary air and exhaust, $\eta \approx 41\%$, $P_{\text{net}} = 2.79\text{ kW}$):
    $$t_{\text{baffle}} = \frac{3,879,000\text{ J}}{2790\text{ W}} = 1390\text{ s} \approx \mathbf{23.2\text{ minutes}}$$

#### 1D Steady-State Wall & Touch Temperatures
Using the benchmark boundary conditions ($T_{\text{gas}} = 500^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{\text{air}} = 20^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta T = 480\text{ K}$, $h_{\text{in}} = 25\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$, $h_{\text{ext}} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$):
*   $R_{\text{in}} = 1 / 25 = 0.0400\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   $R_{\text{SS liner}} = 0.0015 / 16.0 = 0.0001\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   $R_{\text{AES (75 mm)}} = 0.075 / 0.080 = 0.9375\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   $R_{\text{CalSil (50 mm)}} = 0.050 / 0.065 = 0.7692\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   $R_{\text{Blackwood (16 mm)}} = 0.016 / 0.160 = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   $R_{\text{ext}} = 1 / 10 = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   **Total thermal resistance:**
    $$R_{\text{total}} = 0.0400 + 0.0001 + 0.9375 + 0.7692 + 0.1000 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.9468\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$$
*   **Steady wall heat flux:**
    $$q = \frac{480\text{ K}}{1.9468\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}} = \mathbf{246.56\text{ W/m}^2}$$
*   **Wood inner face temperature** (at the CalSil / Blackwood interface):
    $$T_{\text{wood,in}} = T_{\text{air}} + q \times (R_{\text{ext}} + R_{\text{Blackwood}}) = 20 + 246.56 \times (0.1000 + 0.1000) = \mathbf{69.3^\circ\text{C}}$$
    *(Note: Reducing the stave thickness from 20 mm to 16 mm lowers the wood inner face temperature from $74.8^\circ\text{C}$ to $69.3^\circ\text{C}$ because timber is a poorer insulator than AES/CalSil; thinner wood lets heat escape to ambient air faster rather than banking up at the wood inner boundary. This gives a $10.7^\circ\text{C}$ safety margin below the $80^\circ\text{C}$ limit.)*
*   **Wood outer face temperature:**
    $$T_{\text{wood,out}} = T_{\text{air}} + q \times R_{\text{ext}} = 20 + 246.56 \times 0.1000 = \mathbf{44.7^\circ\text{C}}$$
*   **Upper 304 SS dome crown temperature:** Away from the mouth plume ($R_{\text{tot,crown}} = 1.8468\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$, $q = 259.9\text{ W/m}^2$), the crown sits at **$46.0^\circ\text{C}$**; directly above the mouth eyebrow where $450^\circ\text{C}$ exhaust spills out, the stainless lip reaches **$95\text{–}115^\circ\text{C}$**, which is why the entire mouth tunnel, eyebrow, and upper crown are 304 stainless steel with zero timber in the spill path.
*   **Handle temperature:** The turned Blackwood handles sit on $45\text{ mm}$ stainless stand-offs bolted to the $45^\circ\text{C}$ hoops; convective cooling along the brackets holds the wooden grips at **$26\text{–}30^\circ\text{C}$** (ambient-warm to a bare hand).

### 6. Weather, Vibration, Pack-Down, and Cleaning

*   **Weather (Rain on a Tailgate):**
    *   **Zero exposed top end-grain:** The upper ends of all 24 Blackwood staves terminate at $Z = 305\text{ mm}$, tucked $12\text{ mm}$ inside the overhanging rolled drip skirt of the $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless dome crown and sealed against driving rain by a $6\text{ mm}$ high-temp silicone O-ring cord.
    *   **Drained bottom chime:** The lower stave ends sit on a $6\text{ mm}$ closed-cell silicone sponge cord inside the $25\text{ mm}$ stainless base chime rim. Six $\varnothing 8\text{ mm}$ weep slots punched at the bottom of the stainless chime let any rainwater running down the staves drain immediately rather than pooling against the end-grain.
    *   **Moisture barrier:** The $50\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate core is wrapped in overlapped $0.08\text{ mm}$ aluminium foil tape behind the staves, so neither winter humidity nor damp coastal air can wick into the insulation.
*   **Vibration & Sudden Stops (BYD Shark 6 Corrugations):**
    *   **Stone retention:** The $6.10\text{ kg}$ cordierite stone sits inside a $12\text{ mm}$ deep recessed ring welded to the $1.5\text{ mm}$ stainless hearth pan, cushioned underneath by the $50\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate load disk and radially by a $6\text{ mm}$ braided ceramic fibre rope gasket. For severe outback corrugations, the stone lifts straight out through the $400\text{ mm}$ mouth in 5 seconds and rides vertically in its padded canvas tote strapped against the tub wall.
    *   **Insulation retention:** The rigid $50\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate polygon dome locks the $75\text{ mm}$ AES blanket mechanically against the inner stainless liner so the blanket cannot slump or settle under vertical G-loads.
    *   **Emergency braking restraint:** Four ratchet straps clip from the oven’s four $4.0\text{ mm}$ stainless hoop brackets to the Shark 6’s factory tie-down rings, holding the $42.6\text{ kg}$ drum against a $10g$ forward stop without stressing the wood staves.
*   **Pack-Down Routine:**
    *   Shut off the LCC27 bottle valve. Because the outer wood shell runs at $44.7^\circ\text{C}$ and the handles sit at $28^\circ\text{C}$, the exterior can be handled immediately after dinner.
    *   Pull the $430\text{ mm}$ stone out with leather Welding gloves into its canvas heat-proof sleeve so the oven cools in under 25 minutes.
    *   Pull the three spring-ball hitch pins, slide out the three stainless tripod legs, and lift the oven into the Shark 6 tub using the two side bar handles.
*   **On-Site Cleaning (Gas-Only Mode; No Wood Soot):**
    *   We intentionally omit wood-burning hardware: burning timber in a $480\text{ mm}$ chamber leaves ash in the gas burner ports and tars the blonde/golden Blackwood exterior above the mouth.
    *   To clean after cooking, turn the $6.8\text{ kW}$ burner to full for 10 minutes with the preheat baffle on (pyrolysing spilled cheese and semolina on the cordierite deck to white ash at $480^\circ\text{C}$), brush the ash forward out the stainless mouth chute with a brass bristle brush, and wipe the stainless dome crown and oiled Blackwood staves with a damp microfibre cloth.

### 7. Wood Movement Detail

*   **Species & Cut:** **24 staves** of $16\text{ mm}$ thick **quarter-sawn Tasmanian Blackwood** (*Acacia melanoxylon*, dry density $640\text{ kg/m}^3$, radial shrinkage $1.8\%$, tangential shrinkage $4.2\%$). Quarter-sawing puts the radial plane across the $89\text{ mm}$ max stave width.
*   **Calculated Circumferential Movement:** Across a $\pm 4\%$ wood equilibrium moisture content (EMC) swing between a wet Adelaide Hills winter camp ($14\%$ EMC) and a dry $40^\circ\text{C}$ summer day plus $69.3^\circ\text{C}$ inner-face heating ($6\%$ EMC), quarter-sawn Blackwood moves $\approx 0.14\%$ per $1\%$ MC change ($\pm 0.56\%$ total). Around the $2140\text{ mm}$ mean perimeter, the 24 staves will collectively expand and contract by **$\pm 12.0\text{ mm}$** ($\pm 0.50\text{ mm}$ per stave).
*   **Why Glue Is Forbidden & How the Spline Groove Works:** Gluing 24 staves into a rigid ring around an incompressible calcium silicate core guarantees a catastrophic split on the first dry cook. Instead, the staves are **dry-coopered**:
    *   Each stave edge is ripped at a $7.5^\circ$ dihedral angle and carries a $4.0\text{ mm} \times 8.0\text{ mm}$ stopped groove along its length (stopping $25\text{ mm}$ short of both ends so the spline is invisible).
    *   Twenty-four loose $3.8\text{ mm} \times 14.0\text{ mm}$ quarter-sawn Blackwood splines lubricated with dry PTFE/beeswax bridge the joints. Each stave is free to shrink or swell across its width while the splines lock radial alignment and block line-of-sight gaps.
*   **Belleville-Sprung Tension Hoops:**
    *   Each $30\text{ mm} \times 2.0\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless hoop is split into two half-bands joined on the left and right flanks by machined C36000 brass trunnion blocks and M8 $\times 75\text{ mm}$ A2-70 stainless socket cap draw-bolts.
    *   Each draw-bolt passes through a stack of **8 stainless Belleville disc springs** (DIN 2093, $18\text{ mm}$ OD $\times 8.2\text{ mm}$ ID $\times 1.0\text{ mm}$ thick, arranged in series-parallel for $6.5\text{ mm}$ working deflection per side = **$13.0\text{ mm}$ total circumferential take-up per hoop**).
    *   As the timber swells in winter rain or shrinks during a long cook, the hoops breathe radially with the staves under a constant $\sim 2.0\text{ kN}$ clamping preload—no crushed wood fibres when wet, no loose rattling staves when bone-dry.
*   **Mouth Arch Isolation:** Where the stave circle is interrupted by the $400\text{ mm}$ mouth, the two flanking king-staves are captured inside a $15\text{ mm}$ deep U-channel return on the $2.0\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless mouth surround, packed with ceramic fibre tape so the staves can slide $\pm 3\text{ mm}$ tangentially inside the stainless trim without opening a gap.
*   **Finish:** **Zero film-forming varnish, epoxy, or polyurethane.** End grain is hot-soaked in $1:1$ pure tung oil and D-limonene citrus solvent; stave faces receive four hand-rubbed coats of polymerised tung oil blended with $15\%$ carnauba and beeswax. It cannot peel, craze, or blister, and recoats in 5 minutes with an oily rag.

### 9. The Three Worst Things About This Concept

1.  **It is still 42.6 kg without the stone and legs—a mandatory two-person lift into the ute.** Even after stripping out the castable dome, thinning the Blackwood staves to 16 mm, and replacing the upper wood hemisphere with a 1.2 mm stainless crown (saving 31.8 kg over the naive design), the physics of wrapping a 125 mm thick insulation wall around a 480 mm chamber drives the outer diameter to 765 mm. The 50 mm calcium silicate shell alone weighs 10.48 kg because the radius is so large. You cannot carry this oven solo without risking a disc injury.
2.  **The 765 mm footprint is too wide for the BYD Shark 6 tailgate, forcing you to carry and set up a tripod.** Because the Shark 6 tailgate is only 517 mm deep, you cannot simply slide the oven onto the tailgate and light it. Every cook requires pulling three 780 mm stainless legs from their roll, pinning them into the bottom bayonet sockets, and flipping a 42.6 kg drum onto its feet before sliding the 6.10 kg stone inside.
3.  **184 coopered surfaces have to be machined and fitted dry, and the wood near the mouth will darken faster than the rear.** Making 24 barrel-curved, bevel-edged, spline-grooved staves close tightly around a tapered drum requires disciplined setup on the table saw and WorkBee CNC router. Furthermore, even with the 1.2 mm stainless upper crown and 2.0 mm stainless mouth eyebrow deflecting the exhaust plume, radiant heat and steam during pizza launches will age and amber the two Blackwood king-staves flanking the mouth faster than the staves at the back of the oven.

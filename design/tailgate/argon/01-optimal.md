### 1. The Concept: The Angas 430

**The Angas 430** is a circular, double-skin 304 stainless steel gas pizza oven with a 12-gore TIG-fused dome crown, bound at its waist by a tensioned $35 \times 3\text{ mm}$ C26000 cartridge brass cinch band and carried by two lathe-turned recycled jarrah bar handles. Stainless steel over a hybrid $50\text{ mm}$ AES blanket + $25\text{ mm}$ rigid calcium silicate insulation stack won because an all-metal outer skin has no $80^\circ\text{C}$ charring limit: eliminating the $50\text{ mm}$ extra insulation and $20\text{ mm}$ timber stave thickness required to keep a wood shell below $80^\circ\text{C}$ cuts the radial wall thickness from $146.5\text{ mm}$ down to **$77.4\text{ mm}$**, shrinks the plan diameter from $763\text{ mm}$ to **$625\text{ mm}$**, and drops the dry carry mass (stone lifted out) to **$25.76\text{ kg}$** ($31.86\text{ kg}$ with the $\varnothing 430 \times 20\text{ mm}$ circular cordierite stone installed). It shrugs off heavy rain on the back of the BYD Shark 6, never swells or checks across a $2\text{ m}$ circumference, survives corrugated outback tracks without cracking, and disassembles via a single M8 trunnion bolt on the brass waist band for lifetime maintenance.

### 2. The Two Discarded Runners-Up

*   **Runner-Up 1: Coopered Jarrah Shell over Case C Insulation (`hardwood_shell.py`).** To keep the timber inner face at $74.4^\circ\text{C}$ (under the $80^\circ\text{C}$ char limit at $500^\circ\text{C}$ internal gas), a circular wood shell requires $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless + $75\text{ mm}$ AES + $50\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate + $20\text{ mm}$ jarrah = **$146.5\text{ mm}$ radial wall thickness**. Around a $470\text{ mm}$ ID chamber, that wall blew the plan diameter out to **$763\text{ mm}$** and pushed total mass to **$49.8\text{ kg}$**—killing the one-person lift requirement, eating almost two-thirds of the $1224\text{ mm}$ width between the Shark 6 wheel arches, and exposing $2.40\text{ m}$ of edge-glued jarrah staves to cyclic $44^\circ\text{C}$ thermal drying shrinkage against wet tailgate weather.
*   **Runner-Up 2: Castable Refractory Dome inside a One-Piece Spun Metal Clamshell.** A $25\text{ mm}$ dense castable refractory dome ($\rho = 2200\text{ kg/m}^3$) over a $470\text{ mm}$ chamber weighs **$22.4\text{ kg}$** by itself, driving total oven mass to **$54.2\text{ kg}$** and stretching heat-up soak time to **$68\text{ minutes}$** at $5.8\text{ kW}$. Worse, forming a seamless $\varnothing 625\text{ mm}$ outer dome requires a **$>650\text{ mm}$ swing metal spinning lathe**, a machine that neither Makerspace Adelaide nor the university workshop possesses, while the brittle cast dome is prone to fatigue cracking under corrugated road vibration in the ute tub.

### 3. Dimensioned Fabrication Description

*   **Plan Diameter (Shell OD):** **$625\text{ mm}$** true circle ($709\text{ mm}$ maximum width across the two side jarrah carry handles).
*   **Body Height (Base Pan to Dome Apex):** **$315\text{ mm}$** ($380\text{ mm}$ stowed height including the $65\text{ mm}$ lower valve box and stowed rubber/jarrah feet).
*   **Cooking Floor:** **$\varnothing 430\text{ mm} \times 20\text{ mm}$ thick** uncut circular cordierite stone. Fits a $381\text{ mm}$ ($15\text{ inch}$) pizza with $49\text{ mm}$ diametral clearance ($24.5\text{ mm}$ radial clearance all around) for peel turning. Because the stone is a full circle with no notched corners, it resists thermal-shock cracking and can be rotated $180^\circ$ between sessions to equalize wear.
*   **Internal Chamber:** **$\varnothing 470\text{ mm}$ ID** circular $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless liner ($55\text{ mm}$ vertical cylindrical skirt + $110\text{ mm}$ rise 12-gore torispherical dome). The $\varnothing 430\text{ mm}$ stone sits offset **$15\text{ mm}$ to the right (starboard)** of the chamber centerline, leaving a $5\text{ mm}$ expansion gap on the right wall and a **$35\text{ mm}$ wide crescent burner slot** on the left (port) wall.
*   **Internal Apex Height:** **$165\text{ mm}$** above the top face of the cordierite deck.
*   **Mouth Opening:** **$395\text{ mm}$ wide $\times 90\text{ mm}$ high** at the center of the arch ($75\text{ mm}$ high at the side shoulders), centered on the offset $\varnothing 430\text{ mm}$ stone.
*   **Mouth-to-Apex Ratio:** **$90\text{ mm} / 165\text{ mm} = \mathbf{0.545}$** (traps a $75\text{ mm}$ deep buoyant pocket of rolling flame above the mouth soffit).
*   **Wall & Floor Stack:**
    *   *Cylindrical Side Wall ($77.4\text{ mm}$ total):* $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS inner liner + $50\text{ mm}$ AES blanket ($2 \times 25\text{ mm}$, $\rho = 128\text{ kg/m}^3$) + $25\text{ mm}$ segmented calcium silicate board ($\rho = 250\text{ kg/m}^3$, rigid backing to prevent outer skin dents in the ute tub) + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS outer skin.
    *   *Dome Crown ($77.4\text{ mm}$ total):* $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS inner dome + $75\text{ mm}$ AES blanket ($3 \times 25\text{ mm}$ layers) + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS outer dome.
    *   *Floor Stack ($72.7\text{ mm}$ total):* $20\text{ mm}$ cordierite stone + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS stone pan + $25\text{ mm}$ rigid calcium silicate load-bearing board + $25\text{ mm}$ AES blanket + $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 SS structural base pan.
*   **Handles:** Two $\varnothing 32\text{ mm} \times 220\text{ mm}$ turned recycled jarrah bars with lathe-turned C26000 brass end ferrules, stood off $35\text{ mm}$ from the outer cylinder at the vertical center of gravity on four $4\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless brackets isolated with $3\text{ mm}$ PTFE thermal-break washers.
*   **Stowed Envelope in BYD Shark 6 Tub:** **$709\text{ mm}\text{ (W)} \times 625\text{ mm}\text{ (D)} \times 380\text{ mm}\text{ (H)}$**. Fits easily between the $1224\text{ mm}$ wheel arches and clears the $517\text{ mm}$ tub depth (and $460\text{ mm}$ tonneau target) by **$137\text{ mm}$**.
*   **Tailgate vs. Stand Verdict:** At **$625\text{ mm}$** plan diameter, the oven exceeds the $480\text{ mm}$ limit and **cannot sit fully on the $517\text{ mm}$ deep lowered Shark 6 tailgate** (nor would operating on the tailgate satisfy the AS/NZS 5601.1 $300\text{ mm}$ rear clearance from the painted ute body). Instead, three lathe-turned 304 stainless leg sockets welded at $12^\circ$ splay to the $1.5\text{ mm}$ base pan give two standing modes:
    1.  **Camp-Table Mode:** Three stub feet ($\varnothing 50\text{ mm}$ turned jarrah + EPDM rubber pads, $65\text{ mm}$ projection) sit inside a $540\text{ mm}$ pitch circle for a camp table.
    2.  **Free-Standing Ground Tripod Mode:** Three **$750\text{ mm}$ long $\times \varnothing 25.4 \times 1.6\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless tube legs** lock into the base sockets via captive spring-loaded stainless detent pins, placing the cooking stone at **$860\text{ mm}$ standing height** on uneven ground beside the ute while leaving the lowered $517\text{ mm}$ Shark 6 tailgate free as the dough prep and launch bench.

### 5. Heat-Up & Shell Temperature Calculations

#### 1D Steady-State Wall Resistance Sum ($T_{gas} = 500^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{amb} = 20^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta T = 480\text{ K}$)
*   Internal convection ($h_{in} = 25\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$): $R_{in} = 1 / 25 = 0.0400\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
*   External convection + radiation ($h_{ext} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$): $R_{ext} = 1 / 10 = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$

**A. Cylindrical Side Wall ($1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS + $50\text{ mm}$ AES + $25\text{ mm}$ CalSil + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS):**
$$R_{SS,in} = \frac{0.0012}{16.0} = 0.000075\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$$
$$R_{AES} = \frac{0.050}{0.080} = 0.6250\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$$
$$R_{CalSil} = \frac{0.025}{0.065} = 0.3846\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$$
$$R_{SS,out} = \frac{0.0012}{16.0} = 0.000075\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$$
$$R_{total,wall} = 0.0400 + 0.000075 + 0.6250 + 0.3846 + 0.000075 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.1498\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$$
$$q_{wall} = \frac{500 - 20}{1.1498} = \mathbf{417.5\text{ W/m}^2}$$
$$T_{skin,wall} = 20 + 417.5 \times 0.1000 = \mathbf{61.8^\circ\text{C}}$$

**B. Dome Crown ($1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS + $75\text{ mm}$ AES + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS):**
$$R_{AES,crown} = \frac{0.075}{0.080} = 0.9375\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$$
$$R_{total,crown} = 0.0400 + 0.00015 + 0.9375 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.0777\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$$
$$q_{crown} = \frac{480}{1.0777} = \mathbf{445.4\text{ W/m}^2}, \quad T_{skin,crown} = 20 + 445.4 \times 0.1000 = \mathbf{64.5^\circ\text{C}}$$

**C. Jarrah Carry Handles:**
Isolated on $35\text{ mm}$ long $\times 4\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless brackets with $3\text{ mm}$ PTFE washers ($R_{bracket} \approx 18.5\text{ K/W}$ per post), the jarrah bars remain at **$29^\circ\text{C}$** in $20^\circ\text{C}$ air.

#### Thermal Mass & Heat-Up Time to $430^\circ\text{C}$ Top Deck ($410^\circ\text{C}$ Mean Bulk Stone)
1.  **Cordierite Deck ($6.10\text{ kg}$, $c_p = 900\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, $\Delta T_{mean} = 390\text{ K}$):**
    $$Q_{stone} = 6.10 \times 900 \times 390 = \mathbf{2.141\text{ MJ}}$$
2.  **304 Stainless Inner Liner ($4.06\text{ kg}$, $c_p = 500\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, $\Delta T = 460\text{ K}$):**
    $$Q_{liner} = 4.06 \times 500 \times 460 = \mathbf{0.934\text{ MJ}}$$
3.  **Insulation Hot-Half Enthalpy ($6.44\text{ kg}$ total, $c_p = 1000\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, effective heated mass fraction $0.45$ at $\Delta T = 460\text{ K}$):**
    $$Q_{ins} = 0.45 \times 6.44 \times 1000 \times 460 = \mathbf{1.333\text{ MJ}}$$
*   **Total Enthalpy to Soak:** $Q_{total} = 2.141 + 0.934 + 1.333 = \mathbf{4.408\text{ MJ}}$.
*   **Heat-Up Time at $5.8\text{ kW}$ ($20.9\text{ MJ/h}$) Burner Input:** With a mean heat-transfer capture efficiency of $\eta_{net} = 0.45$ into the chamber walls and deck ($\dot{Q}_{net} = 0.45 \times 5800\text{ W} = 2610\text{ W}$, accounting for flue gas spill out the mouth and $\sim 240\text{ W}$ skin loss):
    $$t_{soak} = \frac{4,408,000\text{ J}}{2610\text{ W}} = 1689\text{ s} = \mathbf{28.2\text{ minutes}}$$
    *(Through-thickness diffusion check for $L = 20\text{ mm}$ cordierite, $\alpha = k/\rho c_p = 7.94 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$: $3\tau = 3 L^2 / \alpha = 1511\text{ s} = \mathbf{25.2\text{ minutes}}$, confirming the stone bottom face is thermally soaked when the top face hits $430^\circ\text{C}$).*

### 6. Build Path & The Three Heirloom Details

#### Machine Allocation
*   **Makerspace Adelaide (`Trotec Q500` / `Thunder Nova35` CO2 Lasers):** Cut $3\text{ mm}$ MDF flat-pattern gore templates, radius gauges, and laser-mask the C26000 brass nameplate for acid etching.
*   **Makerspace Adelaide (`WorkBee CNC Router`):** Cut stacked $18\text{ mm}$ MDF male forming bucks for the $\varnothing 470\text{ mm}$ inner and $\varnothing 625\text{ mm}$ outer 12-gore domes, plus the router circle-jig for trimming the $\varnothing 430\text{ mm}$ cordierite stone and $\varnothing 470\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate disc.
*   **Makerspace Adelaide (`Woodworking — Table Saw & Thicknesser`):** Dimension recycled jarrah blanks and cut the $15^\circ$-bevelled $25\text{ mm}$ calcium silicate lagging staves.
*   **Makerspace Adelaide (`Metalworking — Cold Cut Saw, Metal Lathe, Manual Mill`):** Cut $\varnothing 25.4\text{ mm}$ 304 leg tubes; turn the two $\varnothing 20\text{ mm}$ brass cinch-band trunnions, four brass handle ferrules, three $\varnothing 32\text{ mm}$ 304 leg socket bosses, and two $\varnothing 32 \times 220\text{ mm}$ jarrah handle bars; mill the $4\text{ mm}$ 304 handle standoff brackets.
*   **University Workshop (`process: plasma/guillotine profiling, slip-rolling, brake pressing, and purge-TIG welding of 1.2–1.5 mm 304 stainless`):** Cut the 2D stainless blanks from the laser-cut MDF templates; slip-roll the cylindrical skirts and 12 dome gores; fusion-TIG weld the dome seams over the WorkBee MDF bucks; TIG-weld the leg bosses to the $1.5\text{ mm}$ base pan.

#### The Three Details That Make It Look Expensive
1.  **The C26000 Solid Brass Waist Cinch Band & Lathe-Turned Barrel Trunnions:** Rather than welding the outer dome crown to the outer cylindrical skirt (which causes oil-canning distortion across a $1.96\text{ m}$ butt weld and locks the insulation inside forever), the upper dome slips over a $15\text{ mm}$ joggled lip on the lower cylinder. A **$35 \times 3\text{ mm}$ solid C26000 brass hoop** wraps the joint and loops at the rear around two $\varnothing 20 \times 40\text{ mm}$ solid brass barrel trunnions turned and cross-drilled on the Makerspace lathe, tensioned by a single M8 304 socket-head cap screw.
2.  **The 12-Gore Autogenous-TIG Dome with Radial Satin Grain & Peened Brass Badge:** The 12 gores of $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless are developed in Fusion 360, rolled to single curvature at the university workshop, clamped over the CNC-routed MDF buck, and autogenously purge-TIG welded without filler rod build-up. The seams are planished with a hammer and dolly over the buck and blended on a turntable with 180-grit and Maroon Scotch-Brite into a continuous circumferential satin grain, topped above the mouth by an acid-etched $110 \times 35 \times 3\text{ mm}$ brass badge fixed with two solid $4\text{ mm}$ peened brass rivets into the cool outer skin.
3.  **The Slip-Joint Arch Mouth Cowling & Turned Jarrah/Brass Bar Handles:** The $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 mouth tunnel is TIG-fused and radius-blended to the outer shell at the front, while its inner edge engages a $10\text{ mm}$ telescoping slip-flange on the inner liner—absorbing $3.5\text{ mm}$ of differential thermal expansion at $500^\circ\text{C}$ without warping the outer face, and presenting zero exposed sheet edges or fasteners.

### 7. Certified Gas System & Wood-Fire Verdict

*   **Power Band:** **$5.5\text{ to }6.2\text{ kW}$ ($20\text{ to }22\text{ MJ/h}$)** propane at **$2.75\text{ kPa}$** ($\sim 420\text{ g/h}$ LPG).
*   **Why This Power Matches This Floor:** Gozney's Arc Lite uses $3.4\text{ kW}$ for a $0.115\text{ m}^2$ hearth ($29.5\text{ kW/m}^2$) and an $85\text{ mm}$ mouth. Our $\varnothing 430\text{ mm}$ circular floor is **$0.1452\text{ m}^2$** ($1.26\times$ larger) and our $395 \times 90\text{ mm}$ arched mouth is **$0.0324\text{ m}^2$** ($1.45\times$ larger radiation/convection aperture). Scaling input power to **$5.8\text{ kW}$ ($20.9\text{ MJ/h}$)** yields **$39.9\text{ kW/m}^2$** of hearth power density—matching commercial sub-30-minute heat-up and giving a 90-second deck recovery between $15\text{ inch}$ pizzas.
*   **Bought-In Certified Gas Components (Zero Jet Modification):**
    1.  **Complete OEM LPG Burner & FFD Valve Assembly:** A factory-assembled, AGA-approved **Gozney Arc XL / Roccbox replacement LPG burner unit** (or equivalent certified **Bromic / Cobra $21\text{ MJ/h}$ stainless tubular oven burner + SIT/Coprecipel flame-failure gas valve + M8 thermocouple + factory-calibrated $2.75\text{ kPa}$ LPG venturi/injector** bought complete as an untouched OEM spare or harvested intact from a certified commercial salamander/deck oven). Installed in the under-slung 304 stainless manifold box beneath the left-rear crescent port.
    2.  **Regulator & Hose:** Bought new off-the-shelf: **AGA-certified LCC27 $2.75\text{ kPa}$ regulator ($2\text{ kg/h}$ capacity) with $1.2\text{ m}$ stainless-braided Class C hose certified to AS/NZS 1869**.
*   **Wood-Fire Mode Verdict:** **No.** Wood-fire is not free on a $\varnothing 470\text{ mm}$ ID chamber:
    1.  A wood ember bed or basket inside the chamber steals $110\text{ mm}$ of lateral hearth width, reducing the maximum pizza from $381\text{ mm}$ ($15\text{ inch}$) to $270\text{ mm}$ ($10.6\text{ inch}$) unless the shell diameter grows to $735\text{ mm}$ ($+9.8\text{ kg}$).
    2.  A wood fire requires a top flue penetration that vents the buoyant $500^\circ\text{C}$ gas dome pocket when running on LPG, costing $\sim 1.8\text{ kW}$ in ceiling heat loss.
    3.  Fly ash falls directly into the $35\text{ mm}$ crescent gas port, fouling the FFD thermocouple and burner ports.

### 9. What a Careful Owner Will Dislike in Year Five

1.  **Golden-Straw Thermal Patina on the Upper Mouth Arch:** Even with the slip-joint thermal break, the $450^\circ\text{C}$ exhaust plume spilling over the top of the $395\text{ mm}$ mouth will turn the first $40\text{ mm}$ of brushed 304 stainless above the opening a permanent straw-bronze oxide tint and bake on pyrolysis grease from cheese steam. You can scrub it back with Barkeepers Friend or embrace the bronze gradient around the brass badge, but it will never stay uniform silver above the arch.
2.  **Removing the Stone to Clean Under-Deck Crumb Char:** Semolina and burnt flour pushed off the peel eventually fall down the $5\text{ mm}$ right-hand perimeter gap onto the $1.2\text{ mm}$ stainless stone pan. Every 15–20 cooks, you must tilt the $6.10\text{ kg}$ $\varnothing 430\text{ mm}$ stone $22^\circ$ to slide it out the $395\text{ mm}$ mouth and brush out the carbonized semolina.
3.  **Re-Oiling the Jarrah Handles:** Leaving the oven on the camp table in South Australian sun and rain will grey the tung-oiled jarrah handles within 18 months unless they get a 5-minute scotch-brite rub and a fresh wipe of pure tung oil each spring.

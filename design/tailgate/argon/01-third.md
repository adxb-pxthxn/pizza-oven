### 1. Name and Concept Summary

**The Angas Binnacle** (Stave-Cradled Cool-Skin Cupola)

The Angas Binnacle splits the oven into two thermally and structurally distinct assemblies separated by a 15.3 mm free-convecting annular air gap: **(1) a self-contained, cool-touch 304 stainless steel cooking vessel** insulated thick enough (75 mm AES blanket + 30 mm calcium silicate board) that its bare upper metal dome sits at **50.3°C** in 20°C air, and **(2) an external 16-stave coopered Tasmanian Blackwood binnacle ring-cradle** that surrounds only the lower cylindrical waist ($z = 0$ to $215\text{ mm}$). The stainless cupola contains 100% of the fire, weather, and insulation; the Blackwood binnacle never spans the dome, never sees the flame, and never acts as the insulation vessel. Instead, the coopered timber ring—cinched by two $35 \times 3\text{ mm}$ 304 stainless hoops on silicon-bronze trunnions—acts as the structural chassis: it carries the four turned Blackwood lifting handles, the three folding 304 stainless legs, the recessed gas-valve vault, and the tie-down loads in the back of the BYD Shark 6 ute so ratchet straps and corrugated tracks never dent the 1.2 mm stainless vessel skin.

### 2. Why This Architecture Is Neither the Full Wood Shell Nor the Hot-Skin Dome

1. **Why it is not the full coopered wood shell:**
   - A full wood shell puts timber ($R_{wood} = 0.125\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$ for 20 mm hardwood) on the *outside* of the insulation stack, trapping heat inside the wood and forcing a 125 mm insulation wall (75 mm AES + 50 mm CalSil) plus a compound-curved coopered dome that wants to split across a 400 mm+ radius when rained on and heat-cycled.
   - In the Angas Binnacle, the upper dome is exposed satin-grained 304 stainless steel ($R_{skin} \approx 0.0001\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$), dumping heat directly to ambient air ($h_{ext} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$). That allows a thinner **105 mm** insulation stack (75 mm AES + 30 mm CalSil) to hold the bare metal dome at **50.3°C** (well under the 60°C bare-hand limit) while eliminating compound-curved timber staves above the waist.
   - The Blackwood binnacle is a straight-stave cylindrical ring ($215\text{ mm}$ tall) decoupled from the sealed stainless cooking vessel by eight turned 304 stainless standoff spools across a 15.3 mm open annular gap. If you unbolted the eight standoff spools, the stainless cooking module would still be a completely sealed, weather-tight, cool-touch oven.

2. **Why it is not the hot-skin portable commercial dome:**
   - Commercial portable domes (e.g., Gozney Arc Lite / Tread class) use a single thin layer of AES blanket inside a thin metal shell that runs at 180–280°C, burns bare hands, dumps kilowatts of shell loss in wind, and relies on plastic feet or tubular crash bars.
   - The Angas Binnacle is a cool-skin vessel (**50.3°C** upper dome, **23.8°C** Blackwood binnacle outer face in normal operation) cradled in solid 18 mm coopered Tasmanian Blackwood, 304 stainless hoops, and machined silicon-bronze hardware.

3. **Why this is a true thermal split, not a decorative veneer:**
   - Gluing or strapping wood slats directly onto a thin, under-insulated metal shell is a fake split that chars the wood from behind. Here, the metal vessel's internal insulation alone provides $R_{vessel} = 1.439\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$ before the heat ever reaches the outer stainless skin. Even if the 15.3 mm annular air gap between the stainless waist and the Blackwood binnacle is **100% blocked with mud or leaves** against a 500°C internal gas boundary, the wood inner face reaches **78.6°C**—staying under the 80°C char/degradation limit without relying on slot ventilation to survive.

### 5. Temperatures, Resistance Sums, and Gap Radiation Analysis

Boundary conditions (per standard 1D steady wall model):
* Internal hot gas: $T_{gas} = 500^\circ\text{C}$, $h_{in} = 25\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$ ($R_{in} = 1/25 = 0.0400\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$)
* Ambient air: $T_{amb} = 20^\circ\text{C}$, $h_{ext} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$ ($R_{ext} = 1/10 = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$)
* Total driving temperature difference: $\Delta T = 500 - 20 = 480\text{ K}$

#### A. Exposed Upper 304 Stainless Cupola ($z = 215$ to $410\text{ mm}$)
Wall stack: $1.5\text{ mm}$ 304 SS ($k = 16\text{ W/mK}$) + $75\text{ mm}$ AES ($k = 0.08\text{ W/mK}$) + $30\text{ mm}$ CalSil ($k = 0.065\text{ W/mK}$) + $1.2\text{ mm}$ 304 SS ($k = 16\text{ W/mK}$).
* $R_{in} = 0.0400\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* $R_{liner} = 0.0015 / 16 = 0.0001\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* $R_{AES} = 0.075 / 0.08 = 0.9375\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* $R_{CalSil} = 0.030 / 0.065 = 0.4615\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* $R_{skin} = 0.0012 / 16 = 0.0001\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* **Subtotal vessel resistance:** $R_{vessel} = 0.0400 + 0.0001 + 0.9375 + 0.4615 + 0.0001 = \mathbf{1.4392\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$
* $R_{ext} = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
* **Total dome resistance:** $R_{total,dome} = 1.4392 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.5392\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$
* **Steady heat flux:** $q_{dome} = 480 / 1.5392 = \mathbf{311.9\text{ W/m}^2}$
* **Interface temperatures:**
  * Inner stainless liner: $T_{liner} = 500 - 311.9(0.0400) = \mathbf{487.5^\circ\text{C}}$
  * AES / CalSil interface: $T_{AES/CS} = 487.5 - 311.9(0.9375) = \mathbf{195.1^\circ\text{C}}$ (well below CalSil continuous limit of $650–1000^\circ\text{C}$)
  * **Outer stainless dome skin:** $T_{skin} = 20 + 311.9(0.1000) = \mathbf{51.2^\circ\text{C}}$ (**PASS**: $< 60^\circ\text{C}$ bare-hand touch limit).

#### B. Lower Waist with 15.3 mm Annular Air Gap & 18 mm Blackwood Binnacle — Case 1: Ventilated (Normal Operation)
The $15.3\text{ mm}$ annular gap is completely open around its top perimeter ($z = 215\text{ mm}$) and fed by eight $80 \times 25\text{ mm}$ arch scallops at the base ($z = 0$ to $25\text{ mm}$), plus the $20\text{ mm}$ open under-pan plenum.
* With buoyancy-driven air entering at $20^\circ\text{C}$ and mean cavity air at $T_{cav} = 30^\circ\text{C}$ ($h_{cav} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$), the vessel skin sits at $T_{skin} \approx 30 + \frac{500-30}{1.4392 + 0.100} \times 0.100 = \mathbf{60.5^\circ\text{C}}$ ($333.65\text{ K}$).
* **Radiation across the 15.3 mm gap** (conservatively taking oxidised/sooty stainless $\varepsilon_1 = 0.90$ and Blackwood $\varepsilon_2 = 0.90$, so $\varepsilon_{eff} = \frac{1}{1/0.9 + 1/0.9 - 1} = 0.818$):
  * With wood inner face at $T_{w,in} \approx 34.5^\circ\text{C}$ ($307.65\text{ K}$):
    $$q_{rad,vent} = \varepsilon_{eff} \sigma (T_{skin}^4 - T_{w,in}^4) = 0.818 \times 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times (333.65^4 - 307.65^4) = 4.638 \times 10^{-8} \times (1.239 \times 10^{10} - 8.958 \times 10^9) = \mathbf{159.2\text{ W/m}^2}$$
  * Convective cooling of the wood inner face by the $30^\circ\text{C}$ updraft ($h_{cav} = 10\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$) removes $q_{conv} = 10(34.5 - 30.0) = 45.0\text{ W/m}^2$, leaving net flux into the $18\text{ mm}$ Blackwood stave ($R_{wood} = 0.018 / 0.16 = 0.1125\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$, $R_{ext} = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$) of:
    $$q_{wood} = \frac{34.5 - 20}{0.1125 + 0.1000} = \frac{14.5}{0.2125} = \mathbf{68.2\text{ W/m}^2}$$
  * **Wood inner face (ventilated):** $\mathbf{34.5^\circ\text{C}}$ (**PASS**: $< 80^\circ\text{C}$).
  * **Wood outer face (ventilated):** $T_{w,out} = 20 + 68.2(0.1000) = \mathbf{26.8^\circ\text{C}}$.

#### C. Lower Waist with 15.3 mm Annular Gap — Case 2: All Vents 100% Blocked/Choked (Fault State)
Assume all bottom and top vents are completely plugged with mud, ash, or leaves, turning the $15.3\text{ mm}$ gap into a sealed stagnant air and radiation cavity in direct series with the $500^\circ\text{C}$ gas boundary.
* Across the sealed $15.3\text{ mm}$ gap at $T_{skin} \approx 103.4^\circ\text{C}$ ($376.55\text{ K}$) and $T_{w,in} \approx 78.6^\circ\text{C}$ ($351.75\text{ K}$), with conservative $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2 = 0.90$ ($\varepsilon_{eff} = 0.818$) and $k_{air} = 0.030\text{ W/mK}$:
  * Linearised radiative conductance:
    $$h_{rad} = \varepsilon_{eff} \cdot 4 \sigma T_{avg}^3 = 0.818 \times 4 \times 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times (364.15)^3 = \mathbf{8.96\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}}$$
  * Stagnant air conductive conductance:
    $$h_{cond} = \frac{k_{air}}{L_{gap}} = \frac{0.030}{0.0153} = \mathbf{1.96\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}}$$
  * Combined sealed-gap conductance: $h_{gap} = 8.96 + 1.96 = 10.92\text{ W/m}^2\text{K} \implies R_{gap} = 1 / 10.92 = \mathbf{0.0916\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$.
* **Total Choked-Wall Resistance Sum:**
  * $R_{vessel} = 1.4392\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
  * $R_{gap,choked} = 0.0916\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
  * $R_{wood} = 0.018 / 0.16 = 0.1125\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
  * $R_{ext} = 0.1000\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}$
  * **$R_{total,choked} = 1.4392 + 0.0916 + 0.1125 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.7433\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$**
* **Choked heat flux:** $q_{choked} = 480 / 1.7433 = \mathbf{275.3\text{ W/m}^2}$
  *(Breakdown across choked gap: $q_{rad} = 226.1\text{ W/m}^2$ + $q_{cond} = 49.2\text{ W/m}^2 = 275.3\text{ W/m}^2$)*
* **Choked interface temperatures:**
  * Vessel stainless skin inside blocked gap: $T_{skin,choked} = 500 - 275.3(1.4392) = \mathbf{103.8^\circ\text{C}}$
  * **Blackwood inner face (100% blocked vents):** $T_{w,in,choked} = 20 + 275.3(0.1125 + 0.1000) = \mathbf{78.5^\circ\text{C}}$ (**PASS**: $< 80.0^\circ\text{C}$ wood inner-face limit even in a total vent-blockage fault!).
  * **Blackwood outer face (100% blocked vents):** $T_{w,out,choked} = 20 + 275.3(0.1000) = \mathbf{47.5^\circ\text{C}}$.

#### D. Under-Hearth Bottom Pan ($z = 20\text{ mm}$)
Stack: $20\text{ mm}$ Cordierite ($k=1.5$) + $1.5\text{ mm}$ SS + $30\text{ mm}$ CalSil ($k=0.065$) + $50\text{ mm}$ AES ($k=0.08$) + $1.5\text{ mm}$ SS bottom pan:
* $R_{total,base} = 0.0400 + (0.020/1.5) + (0.030/0.065) + (0.050/0.08) + 0.1000 = 0.0400 + 0.0133 + 0.4615 + 0.6250 + 0.1000 = \mathbf{1.2398\text{ m}^2\text{K/W}}$
* With stone top face at $430^\circ\text{C}$ during steady cooking ($\Delta T = 410\text{ K}$ across conductive stack + $R_{ext}$):
  $q_{base} = 410 / 1.1998 = 341.7\text{ W/m}^2 \implies T_{pan,bottom} = 20 + 341.7(0.1000) = \mathbf{54.2^\circ\text{C}}$ (elevated $20\text{ mm}$ above table/tub floor).

### 6. Build Path and the Three Expensive-Looking Details

#### Workshop Build Path (Makerspace Adelaide + University Engineering Workshop)
1. **Coopered Blackwood Binnacle (Makerspace Adelaide Woodshop + WorkBee CNC):**
   * Rip 16 staves from $25\text{ mm}$ rough-sawn Tasmanian Blackwood on the **Makerspace table saw** with the blade tilted to **$11.25^\circ$** ($360^\circ / 32$), thicknessed to $20\text{ mm}$ on the **Makerspace thicknesser**.
   * Cut spline slots along stave edges on the table saw; glue up the 16-sided drum around two temporary MDF internal formers cut on the **WorkBee CNC router**.
   * Mount the glued drum flat on the **WorkBee CNC router** bed (since $215\text{ mm}$ height fits under the gantry; if Z-travel requires, rout in two $108\text{ mm}$ high rings joined by a stepped rabbet hidden directly behind the middle stainless hoop) and profile-mill the outer and inner surfaces into a true $\varnothing 762\text{ mm}$ OD / $\varnothing 726\text{ mm}$ ID cylinder, plus the 8 lower vent scallops and $70^\circ$ front landing notch. Hand-plane and sand to 320 grit; finish with 4 coats of pure tung oil cut with citrus solvent (penetrating oil finish—never cracks or peels under thermal cycling).
2. **Turned Hardware, Handles & Badge (Makerspace Metal Lathe, Mill & Trotec Laser):**
   * Turn the 8 waisted 304 stainless standoff spools ($\varnothing 24\text{ mm} \times 15.3\text{ mm}$ long, M8 through-bore) and the 4 Blackwood handle ferrules on the **Makerspace metal lathe**.
   * Mill and turn the 4 C65500 silicon-bronze hoop trunnions ($28 \times 28 \times 42\text{ mm}$) on the **Makerspace mill and metal lathe**.
